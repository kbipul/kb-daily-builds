## Issues found & actions taken

### A. Day 022 `token-clock` — a test that could not fail *(fixed, pushed)*

Found by opening the live demo rather than re-reading the repo. Three linked problems:

1. **Stale headline.** The page `<title>` and on-page tagline both read *"what **time**
   you run it"* — the pre-revision framing — sitting directly above the amber banner
   announcing that the lever had moved to the **day** ("the cheapest lever is no longer
   run this batch at 3 a.m. — it is run it on Saturday"). The README and `project.json`
   had been updated on 1 Sep; the app had not. It contradicted itself one line apart.

2. **Two identical numbers, one labelled smaller.** The ledger showed *Move deferrable
   work to the weekend: $792.00* beside *Or shift within the day: $792.00 · **the smaller
   lever***. Not a rounding coincidence — the difference is `0.000000`. Root cause is
   structural and interesting: both levers reach the **same ceiling**, every deferrable
   token billed at the off-peak rate, and there is no third cheaper rate to reach. So
   wherever intra-day shifting is unconstrained, the two tie exactly. "The smaller lever"
   was a hard-coded string asserting a comparison nobody computed.

3. **The guard test was hollow.** The rationale and README both cited
   *"beats intra-day shifting on flat round-the-clock batch traffic"* in `weekend.test.ts`
   as proof the claim "cannot rot". That test passed `{}` as the shifted series, so
   `shiftDeferrable` **never ran** — it compared the weekend plan against a zero intra-day
   saving computed from a differently-built baseline. It could not fail. Rewritten to
   actually run the shift, **it fails**: the levers tie at every capacity setting tested.

**The correction is a better claim than the original.** The weekend move's advantage is
**reachability, not magnitude**: it needs no burst headroom and no shift window, so it
reaches the shared ceiling in cases where intra-day shifting cannot get near it. Four
tests now pin both regimes — the tie when shifting is unconstrained, and the win at a
1-hour shift window, at 1.05× burst headroom, and for a spike buried inside 06:00–10:00
UTC where shifting saves **nothing at all** ($0.00) and the weekend move saves in full.

Fixes: title and tagline corrected; a tested `leverNote()` helper now computes the
relation instead of asserting it; the hollow test replaced by four real ones; README's
*"the saving is also simply larger"* replaced with a signed correction paragraph.
Tests 93 → 98, tsc clean, smoke PASS, secret scan PASS.

> **Lesson worth folding into the playbook.** Day 024 caught its own on-screen
> contradiction by opening its published demo the same day. Day 022 shipped one because
> nobody looked at the running page. A test asserting a README claim is only worth the
> assertion it actually makes — this one named the right behaviour in its title and
> tested something else entirely.

### B. New pre-push gate: `scripts/validate-state.mjs` *(added, pushed)*

A `state.json` entry with `status: "built"` and no `"folder"` field makes `publish.yml`
skip the project. From the loop's side the push succeeds and the Action logs an error
nobody reads. **This has now landed twice** — Day 021 (2026-07-28, killed the entire
fan-out) and again on Day 026 (2026-09-06, contained to one project by W33's hardening,
but still needing a follow-up commit.)

W33's hardening was the right fix at the wrong layer: it catches the malformed entry
*after* the push. This validator catches it *before*, which is the only point where the
loop can still act. It checks the `folder` field and that the directory exists, the
`NNN-` prefix against the day number, `demo` enum, duplicate repo names and days,
`dayCounter` against the highest day, and — for `pages` demos — that `vite.config.ts`
carries the matching `base` path.

Verified both ways: passes clean on current state (26 projects, 26 live repos), and
correctly fails with exit 1 when Day 026's `folder` field is removed to simulate the bug.

Recommend adding to PLAYBOOK §2 Step 4 as a mandatory gate before `git push`.

### The finding that matters: 26 repos, 0 stars, 2 followers

Sorting the profile by stargazers returns no counts at all. Every repo is at zero. The
follower count is 2.

The playbook asks whether timely picks "actually land (stars, demo traffic, still relevant
by Sunday)". Three weeks of audits have answered the third clause and quietly skipped the
first two. Answered plainly: **the picks stay relevant and they do not land, because
nothing in the loop distributes them.**

This is not a scoring-weights problem, and it is important not to misread it as one.
Timeliness and Distinctiveness are doing their job — the builds genuinely are same-day and
genuinely are novel. But every one of the four rubric dimensions measures *the artefact*,
and the artefact reaching an audience is not an artefact property. The loop has generated
five weeks of LinkedIn drafts (W30, W32, W33, W35, and this one) and there is no evidence
any has been posted. A same-day tool riding a two-day-old signal that nobody is told about
is worth less than a week-old tool that 500 people see.

**Recommendation to Bipul — one change, not a rubric rewrite.** Do not add a fifth scoring
dimension; that would just make the loop argue with itself. Instead close the loop's
output end: pick *one* channel and make posting a step rather than an artefact. The
cheapest version is that Monday's dispatch summary includes the previous week's best draft
ready to paste, and the following audit records whether it went out. Until something
measures reception, every future audit's selection-quality section is scoring a rehearsal.

The 6-month goal is to be an authentic AI/ML voice in India. Twenty-six repos is
comfortably enough evidence. Zero followers is the actual bottleneck, and it is the one
thing the daily loop cannot fix by building better.
